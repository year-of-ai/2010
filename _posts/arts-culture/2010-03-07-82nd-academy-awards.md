**Key figures**: Kathryn Bigelow (Best Director), Mark Boal (producer and screenwriter, *The Hurt Locker*), James Cameron (nominee, *Avatar*), Jeff Bridges and Sandra Bullock (lead acting winners), Christoph Waltz and Mo'Nique (supporting acting winners), Steve Martin and Alec Baldwin (hosts)

## Summary

The 82nd Academy Awards ceremony was held on March 7, 2010, at the Kodak Theatre in Hollywood, Los Angeles, honoring films released in 2009. The evening was co-hosted by Steve Martin and Alec Baldwin. Its dominant story was the contest between two films directed by former spouses: Kathryn Bigelow's low-budget Iraq War drama *The Hurt Locker* and James Cameron's record-breaking science-fiction epic *Avatar*.

*The Hurt Locker* won six awards, including Best Picture, Best Director for Bigelow, and Best Original Screenplay for Mark Boal, along with prizes for film editing, sound editing, and sound mixing. *Avatar*, which had become the highest-grossing film in history, won three technical awards — Best Cinematography, Best Art Direction, and Best Visual Effects. In the acting categories, Jeff Bridges won Best Actor for *Crazy Heart*, Sandra Bullock won Best Actress for *The Blind Side*, Christoph Waltz won Best Supporting Actor for *Inglourious Basterds*, and Mo'Nique won Best Supporting Actress for *Precious*. The ceremony was also the first in more than six decades to feature ten Best Picture nominees rather than five, a change the Academy had announced the previous year.

## Significance

Bigelow's win made her the first woman to receive the Academy Award for Best Director in the eighty-two-year history of the awards, a milestone widely noted given how few women had even been nominated in the category. That a modestly budgeted, independently financed war film prevailed over the year's most commercially successful production was read as an affirmation of directorial craft over box-office scale.

The expansion of the Best Picture field to ten nominees, adopted partly in response to criticism that popular films such as *The Dark Knight* had been overlooked, reshaped Oscar campaigning and remained influential in subsequent years. The 82nd ceremony thus marked both a historic breakthrough for women filmmakers and a structural shift in how the Academy's top honor was contested.
