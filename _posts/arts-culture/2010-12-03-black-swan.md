## Summary

*Black Swan* is a psychological thriller directed by Darren Aronofsky and set in the world of New York City ballet. The film stars Natalie Portman as Nina Sayers, a technically precise but emotionally repressed dancer cast in the dual lead of *Swan Lake*, whose pursuit of perfection and rivalry with a new dancer (Mila Kunis) precipitate a psychological unraveling that blurs the line between reality and hallucination. The cast includes Vincent Cassel as the company's director and Barbara Hershey as Nina's controlling mother.

The film had its world premiere at the 67th Venice International Film Festival on September 1, 2010, where it opened the festival. It then received a limited US theatrical release beginning December 3, 2010, before expanding to a wide release on December 17, 2010. A critical and commercial success, *Black Swan* was produced on a modest budget yet grossed well over 300 million US dollars worldwide, becoming one of Aronofsky's most widely seen works.

## Significance

*Black Swan* was among the most acclaimed films of the 2010–2011 awards season. It earned five Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director, and won the Academy Award for Best Actress for Natalie Portman, whose intensive preparation and physically demanding performance were widely praised. Portman also won the Golden Globe and BAFTA awards for the role.

The film cemented Aronofsky's reputation for psychologically punishing character studies and drew attention to the physical and mental toll of elite ballet. Its stylized treatment of obsession, artistic ambition, and the disintegration of identity made it a cultural touchstone of 2010 cinema and a recurring reference point in discussions of the psychological-thriller genre.
